Making local economies visible to AI

Ask an AI assistant what is on in Bridgetown tonight, or which shop has a cordless drill in stock, and it will guess. The answers exist. They are on the radio, on store websites and in social posts, in no form a machine can use. VeriSitu collects that information, connects it and keeps it current, with every fact linked to where it came from.

The problem

Much of what is true about a place today was said out loud this morning. A radio host mentions that a show has moved. A hardware store changes a price on its website. An organiser posts a new start time. People who live there pick this up without trying.

Software does not. Search engines index pages, and most of this was never a page. AI models learn from data that is months old, in which local detail was thin to begin with. So the systems people now ask first are at their weakest on local, current questions.

For a visitor that means a wasted trip. For a tourism authority, a hotel group or an investor, it means decisions made on an outdated picture.

How it works

VeriSitu runs continuously. It listens to live radio, reads local news and store websites, and follows public social channels. From each it extracts the facts: who, what, where, when and how much.

It then matches those facts against what it already holds. Three stations talking about the same cricket match become one event with three sources. A changed start time replaces the old one, and the old one stays on record. The result is a current, structured account of a place that an application, a search box or an AI agent can query.

Applications

Pulse

Pulse answers questions about what is happening in Barbados. It listens to ten FM stations, reads the news and follows TikTok and Instagram. Every answer comes with the sources behind it, including radio clips you can play.

pulsebarbados.com

In beta. Built with tourism and hospitality in mind.

Yuh Gettin’ Tru?

Yuh Gettin’ Tru? is one search across the island’s shops. It scans store websites daily and understands Bajan, US and UK names for the same product. At the time of writing it covers 501,597 products and services from 132 stores and 2,054 vendors.

What people search for and fail to find is, in aggregate, a record of unmet demand.

yuhgettintru.com

In beta. For shoppers, retailers and distributors.

We Outside 246

We Outside is a guide to parties, concerts, fetes and limes in Barbados. It finds events automatically from the Instagram posts of organisers and promoters, credits them, and links every listing back to the original post.

At the time of writing it has scanned 53,167 posts from 665 profiles. Of those, 6,386 were event posters, which it has reconciled into 1,514 distinct events.

weoutside246.com

For anyone deciding where to go out.

Sources

An answer is only as good as your ability to check it. Every fact VeriSitu holds stays linked to the broadcast, page or post it came from, and to when that was published.

If two sources disagree, both are shown. When something changes, the earlier version is kept and marked as replaced. Search activity is only ever used in aggregate.
